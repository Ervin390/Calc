# How to Convert Minutes to Decimal Hours for Payroll

Payroll software and accounting systems require employee time input in decimal format rather than standard hours and minutes. Entering 7 hours and 30 minutes as 7.3 hours causes significant payroll underpayments over time, because 30 minutes equals 0.5 hours.

This guide explains how to convert clock minutes to decimal hours using the simple mathematical formula, standard quarter-hour rounding rules, and reference conversion charts for accurate payroll management using our [<a href="/hours-calculator/">Hours Calculator</a>](/hours-calculator/).

---

## The Decimal Conversion Formula

To convert minutes to decimal hours, divide the total number of minutes by 60:

$$\text{Decimal Hours} = \frac{\text{Minutes}}{60}$$

### Worked Example:

An employee works 8 hours and 45 minutes on Tuesday.

$$\text{Decimal Minutes} = \frac{45}{60} = 0.75$$

Total payroll time: **8.75 hours**.

If the employee earns $20.00 per hour:
- **Correct Payroll Calculation (8.75 hrs x $20.00):** **$175.00**
- **Incorrect Entry Error (8.45 hrs x $20.00):** **$169.00** *(Underpaid by $6.00 on a single shift)*

## The 7-Minute Rounding Rule (FLSA 15-Minute Rule)

Under the United States Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), employers can round employee time in 15-minute increments:

- **1 to 7 minutes:** Round down to 0.00 hours.
- **8 to 14 minutes:** Round up to 0.25 hours (15 minutes).

```
Clock-in at 8:07 AM -> Rounded to 8:00 AM (8.00 hrs)
Clock-in at 8:08 AM -> Rounded to 8:15 AM (8.25 hrs)
```
