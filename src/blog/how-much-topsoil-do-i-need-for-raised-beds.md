<h1>How Much Topsoil Do I Need for Raised Beds & Gardens?</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 17, 2026 · 6 min read</p>

<p>Building raised garden beds is one of the most effective ways to grow vegetables, herbs, and flowers in healthy soil. However, filling empty raised bed frames requires calculating three-dimensional soil volume. Buying topsoil by trial and error often results in multiple trips to the garden center or half-filled planters.</p>

<p>Calculating raised bed soil requires measuring length, width, and depth in feet, then converting the total cubic feet into cubic yards or retail soil bags.</p>

<h2>Step 1: Calculate Raised Bed Volume in Cubic Feet</h2>

<p>Multiply bed length by width by depth (converted from inches to feet by dividing depth by 12):</p>

<div style="background: var(--card-bg, #f8fafc); border: 1px solid var(--border, #e2e8f0); padding: 1.25rem; border-radius: 8px; font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.05rem; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  Volume (cu ft) = Bed Length (ft) x Bed Width (ft) x [Soil Depth (in) / 12]
</div>

<p>Let us calculate a standard 4x8-foot raised vegetable bed filled to a 12-inch soil depth:</p>

<ul>
    <li>Length: 8 feet</li>
    <li>Width: 4 feet</li>
    <li>Depth: 12 inches / 12 = 1.0 foot</li>
    <li>Total Volume: 8 x 4 x 1.0 = <strong>32 Cubic Feet</strong></li>
</ul>

<h2>Step 2: Convert Cubic Feet to Bag Counts</h2>

<p>Retail stores sell bagged topsoil in 0.75 cubic foot bags and enriched garden soil in 1.0 cubic foot bags. Divide your cubic feet volume by the bag size:</p>

<ul>
    <li><strong>0.75 cu ft Topsoil Bags:</strong> 32 / 0.75 = 42.6 $\rightarrow$ <strong>Buy 43 Bags</strong></li>
    <li><strong>1.0 cu ft Garden Soil Bags:</strong> 32 / 1.0 = 32.0 $\rightarrow$ <strong>Buy 32 Bags</strong></li>
</ul>

<h2>Step 3: Convert Cubic Feet to Bulk Cubic Yards</h2>

<p>If you are filling multiple raised beds, ordering bulk topsoil delivered by dump truck is significantly cheaper than buying individual plastic bags. One cubic yard contains 27 cubic feet:</p>

<p>32 cu ft / 27 cu ft per yard = <strong>1.19 Cubic Yards</strong> of soil.</p>

<h2>Recommended Raised Bed Soil Blend Recipe</h2>

<p>Do not fill raised beds with 100 percent heavy topsoil from local soil yards, which compacts easily and hampers root aeration. Experienced gardeners use a balanced 3-part soil blend:</p>

<ul>
    <li><strong>50% Quality Topsoil:</strong> Provides bulk weight, mineral minerals, and root anchor structure.</li>
    <li><strong>30% Organic Compost:</strong> Provides plant nutrition, beneficial microbes, and water absorption.</li>
    <li><strong>20% Peat Moss or Coir with Perlite:</strong> Improves soil drainage, lightness, and moisture retention.</li>
</ul>
