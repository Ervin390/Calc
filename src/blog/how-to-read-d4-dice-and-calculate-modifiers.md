# How to Read D4 Dice and Calculate Modifiers

In tabletop role-playing games like Dungeons & Dragons (D&D 5e), the four-sided die (**d4**) is frequently used for light weapon damage (like daggers and darts) and healing spells like *Cure Wounds* or *Healing Word*.

Because a tetrahedron (pyramid) die does not land flat with a face pointing directly upward, beginners often wonder how to read the result. This guide explains how to read both top-read and bottom-read d4 dice using our [<a href="/dice-roller/">Dice Roller</a>](/dice-roller/).

---

## 2 Types of Physical D4 Dice

### 1. Top-Read D4 Dice (Most Common Modern Design)
On top-read dice, three numbers appear on each triangular face. When the die settles on the table, look at the **top apex point** of the pyramid. The number printed upright at the top point on all visible faces is your roll result.

### 2. Bottom-Read D4 Dice (Older Classic Design)
On bottom-read dice, numbers are printed along the bottom edge of each face. When the die lands, read the number printed along the **bottom base edge** touching the table surface.

```
Top-Read Die:    Read the upright number at the TOP vertex.
Bottom-Read Die: Read the number printed along the BASE edge.
```

---

## Adding Ability Modifiers ($d4 + X$)

In D&D 5e, attack and spell damage rolls require adding an ability modifier (such as Strength, Dexterity, or Spellcasting modifier) to the die roll result.

$$\text{Total Result} = \text{d4 Die Roll} + \text{Ability Modifier}$$

### Worked Example: Dagger Attack

A Rogue with a **+3 Dexterity modifier** hits a goblin with a dagger ($1d4 + 3$):
1. **Roll the d4 die:** The top vertex shows **2**.
2. **Add Modifier:** $2 + 3 = \mathbf{5 \text{ Piercing Damage}}$.
