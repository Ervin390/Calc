<h1>How to Calculate Rebar for a Concrete Slab</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 5 min read</p>

<p>Concrete offers high compressive strength, but its tensile strength is relatively weak. Placing steel reinforcing bar (rebar) in a grid pattern inside a concrete slab prevents cracking and structural failure under heavy loads like vehicles or patio furniture.</p>

<p>Calculating rebar requirements involves determining the number of long parallel runs and perpendicular cross runs needed based on your grid spacing.</p>

<h2>Step-by-Step Rebar Grid Calculation</h2>

<p>Consider a 12 ft by 16 ft concrete patio slab using #4 rebar (0.5-inch diameter) spaced on a standard 12-inch grid pattern, keeping a 3-inch clearance from slab edges.</p>

<ol>
    <li><strong>Long Runs (16 ft length):</strong> (12 ft width / 1 ft spacing) + 1 = <strong>13 parallel bars</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Cross Runs (12 ft width):</strong> (16 ft length / 1 ft spacing) + 1 = <strong>17 parallel bars</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Total Linear Feet:</strong> (13 x 16 ft) + (17 x 12 ft) = 208 + 204 = <strong>412 linear feet</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Standard 20ft Stick Count:</strong> 412 / 20 = 20.6 sticks (round up to <strong>21 sticks</strong>)</li>
</ol>

<h2>Calculating Steel Weight</h2>

<p>Steel rebar weight is standardized by size class. Standard #4 rebar weighs 0.668 pounds per linear foot. Multiply 412 linear feet by 0.668 to get a total steel reinforcement weight of <strong>275.2 pounds</strong>.</p>
