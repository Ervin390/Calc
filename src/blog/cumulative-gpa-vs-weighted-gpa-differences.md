# Cumulative GPA vs Weighted GPA Differences

When reviewing high school transcripts or filling out college admissions applications (such as the Common App), students encounter terms like **Cumulative GPA**, **Weighted GPA**, and **Unweighted GPA**.

Misunderstanding how these numbers differ can lead to confusion during admissions evaluation. This guide explains the core differences between cumulative and weighted GPAs and how colleges recalculate scores using our [<a href="/gpa-calculator/">GPA Calculator</a>](/gpa-calculator/).

---

## Core Definitions

1. **Cumulative GPA:** Represents your overall grade point average across your entire academic history (e.g., all 4 years of high school or all undergraduate semesters combined). A GPA can be both cumulative and weighted, or cumulative and unweighted.
2. **Unweighted GPA:** Evaluates all classes on a standard 4.0 scale regardless of course difficulty ($A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0.0$).
3. **Weighted GPA:** Awards additional grade points for advanced coursework such as Honors, Advanced Placement (AP), or International Baccalaureate (IB) classes, often scaling up to 5.0 ($AP/IB \; A = 5.0, Honors \; A = 4.5$).

---

## Direct Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Cumulative Unweighted GPA | Cumulative Weighted GPA |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Max Possible Scale** | 4.00 | 5.00 (or higher) |
| **Honors / AP Weighting** | No extra points awarded | Adds +0.5 to +1.0 points for advanced courses |
| **Primary Purpose** | Standardized measure of grade consistency | Reflects academic rigor and course difficulty |
| **Used By Colleges** | Main metric for raw comparison | Used to evaluate course difficulty |

---

## How Colleges Recalculate Your GPA

Admissions offices frequently recalculate incoming high school GPAs to establish a standardized baseline across different grading systems:

- **Unweighting:** Colleges strip school-specific weighting bonuses to evaluate every applicant on a pure 4.0 scale.
- **Core Academic Focus:** Admissions officers often recalculate the GPA using only core academic subjects (English, Math, Science, Social Studies, Foreign Languages), excluding physical education and electives.
