# Why Was Morse Code Invented and How It Works

Invented in the late 1830s by **Samuel Morse** and **Alfred Vail**, Morse code revolutionized global long-distance communications by enabling messages to travel instantaneously over electrical telegraph wires.

Before Morse code, transmitting news across continents took weeks or months by ship and horse. ## Why Morse Code Was Invented

In 1825, Samuel Morse received a letter informing him that his wife was ill. By the time he traveled home, she had already died and been buried. Distracted by how slowly information traveled, Morse dedicated himself to creating a rapid long-distance electrical communication system.

While electric telegraph wires could transmit electrical pulses, scientists lacked a simple standardized code to turn electrical clicks into human readable text. Morse and Vail solved this by mapping letters of the English alphabet to distinct combinations of short pulses (**dots** or *dits*) and long pulses (**dashes** or *dahs*).

---

## How Morse Code Timing Works

The timing of Morse code is built around the duration of a single dot:

1. **Dot Length:** 1 unit of time.
2. **Dash Length:** 3 units of time (3 times longer than a dot).
3. **Intra-character Gap:** 1 unit of silence between dots and dashes within the same letter.
4. **Inter-character Gap:** 3 units of silence between letters.
5. **Word Gap:** 7 units of silence between words.

```
Letter 'A':  • - (dot, dash)
Letter 'S':  • • • (3 dots)
Letter 'O':  - - - (3 dashes)
SOS Signal:  • • •   - - -   • • •
```

---

## Why 'E' and 'T' Have the Shortest Codes

Morse and Vail analyzed frequency charts in local newsrooms to determine which letters appeared most often in the English language:
- **Most frequent letter ('E'):** Encoded as a single dot (`•`).
- **Second most frequent letter ('T'):** Encoded as a single dash (`-`).
- **Rare letters ('Q', 'Z'):** Given longer 4-symbol sequences (`- - • -` and `- - • •`).
