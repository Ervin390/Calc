<h1>How Net 30 Payment Terms Work with Business Days</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 17, 2026 · 6 min read</p>

<p>If you run a small business, work as a freelancer, or handle accounts payable, you deal with trade credit terms daily. One of the most common terms on invoices is <strong>Net 30</strong>. But when an invoice states Net 30, does that mean 30 calendar days or 30 business days?</p>

<p>The standard commercial rule is simple: <strong>Net 30 means 30 calendar days from the invoice date</strong>, including weekends and holidays. However, if the 30th calendar day lands on a weekend or bank holiday, the legal due date shifts to the next business day.</p>

<h2>What Does Net 30 Mean on an Invoice?</h2>

<p>Net 30 is a net payment arrangement where the client or buyer must settle their invoice in full within 30 days of the invoice issuance date. Merchants offer net terms as a form of trade credit to encourage larger orders and build long-term business relationships.</p>

<p>Here is how key trade credit variations differ:</p>

<ul>
    <li><strong>Net 30:</strong> Full payment is due within 30 calendar days.</li>
    <li><strong>2/10 Net 30:</strong> The customer receives a 2 percent cash discount if paid within 10 days; otherwise, full net payment is due in 30 days.</li>
    <li><strong>Net 30 EOM:</strong> Payment is due 30 days after the End of the Month (EOM) in which the invoice was issued.</li>
    <li><strong>Net 14 or Net 60:</strong> Similar rules, but with 14 or 60 calendar days respectively.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Do Weekends and Holidays Count in Net 30?</h2>

<p>Yes, standard calendar days count toward the 30-day window. For instance, if an invoice is dated March 1st, full payment is due on March 31st regardless of how many Saturdays or Sundays occurred in March.</p>

<p>The primary exception occurs when the 30th day falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or official bank holiday. Under contract law, when a payment deadline lands on a non-business day, payment is due on the immediately following working business day without incurring late fees.</p>

<h2>How to Calculate Your Net 30 Due Date</h2>

<p>To avoid dispute over invoice timing, follow this straightforward calculation process:</p>

<ol>
    <li>Note the official invoice date printed on the document.</li>
    <li>Add 30 calendar days to the invoice date.</li>
    <li>Check if the resulting target date lands on a Saturday, Sunday, or national bank holiday.</li>
    <li>If it lands on a weekend or holiday, roll the official due date forward to Monday or the next business morning.</li>
</ol>
