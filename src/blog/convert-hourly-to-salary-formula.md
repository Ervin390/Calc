<h1>How to Convert Hourly Wage to Annual Salary: The Simple Formula</h1>
<p style="color:#6b7280; font-size:0.9rem;">Published August 9, 2026 · 4 min read</p>

<p>Comparing hourly jobs to salaried positions can feel like comparing apples to oranges. Most companies quote salary offers on an annual basis, while retail, hospitality, and trade roles usually offer an hourly wage. Knowing the conversion formula helps you compare offers and make informed career moves.</p>

<h2>The Standard Conversion Formula</h2>

<p>Most full-time workers in the US are scheduled for 40 hours per week, 52 weeks per year. Multiply those two numbers together and you get 2,080 working hours in a standard year.</p>

<p>To convert your hourly rate to an annual salary, multiply the rate by 2,080.</p>

<p><strong>Annual Salary = Hourly Wage x 2,080</strong></p>

<p>For example, if you earn $20 per hour:</p>
<p>$20 x 2,080 = $41,600 per year before taxes.</p>

<h2>The Quick Mental Math Trick</h2>

<p>If you are in the middle of a job interview and need to convert a number instantly, there is a simple shortcut. Double the hourly rate and add three zeros.</p>

<p>Take $25 per hour. Double it to get 50. Add three zeros to get $50,000.</p>

<p>This shortcut assumes exactly 2,000 working hours per year instead of 2,080. It underestimates your actual salary by about 4%, which serves as a helpful safety buffer for taxes and unpaid time off.</p>

<h2>Adjusting for Custom Work Schedules</h2>

<p>The standard 2,080 baseline fails if you work part-time, seasonal hours, or contract schedules. In those situations, you must count your expected annual working hours manually.</p>

<p><strong>Annual Working Hours = Hours per week x Weeks worked per year</strong></p>

<p>If you work 30 hours per week and plan to take 4 weeks of unpaid vacation, you will work 48 weeks. Your annual hours total 1,440. Multiply that by a $25 hourly rate to get an annual salary of $36,000.</p>

<h2>Why Understanding the Conversion Matters</h2>

<p>A salary offer might sound impressive at first, but its value drops if the job expects 50 or 60 hours per week. Convert that salary back to an hourly rate to check if you are still making a fair wage. Being able to run these numbers quickly puts you in a much stronger position during salary negotiations.</p>
