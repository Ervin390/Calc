<h1>How Mortgage Overpayments Cut Years Off Your Loan</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 6 min read</p>

<p>During the early years of a 30-year home mortgage, over 70% of every monthly payment goes straight toward interest charges rather than building principal home equity. Making extra monthly overpayments goes 100% toward reducing your principal balance, compounding interest savings across the remaining loan life.</p>

<p>Calculating the impact of mortgage overpayments shows how small extra contributions shave years off your payoff date.</p>

<h2>Worked Example of a $250,000 Home Loan</h2>

<p>Consider a homeowner with a $250,000 mortgage at a fixed interest rate of 6.0% with 25 years (300 months) remaining. The standard principal and interest payment is $1,610.75 per month.</p>

<p>Look at what happens when adding just <strong>$200.00 extra per month</strong> designated toward principal:</p>

<ul>
    <li><strong>Standard Total Interest Paid:</strong> $233,225.00 over 25 years</li>
    <li><strong>Accelerated Total Interest Paid:</strong> $170,410.00 with overpayments</li>
    <li><strong>Total Interest Saved:</strong> <strong>$62,815.00</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Time Saved Off Loan Term:</strong> 5 years and 4 months shaves off payoff date</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why Early Overpayments Count Most</h2>

<p>Because interest compounds on your remaining principal balance, overpayments made in years 1 through 10 eliminate decades of future compounding interest. Even modest extra payments of $50 or $100 per month make a massive long-term difference.</p>
