<h1>Why Keeping SEO Meta Descriptions Under 160 Characters is Crucial for Rankings</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published {{ date | readableDate }} · 3 min read</p>

<p>The meta description is often treated as an afterthought. You write the page, you write the title, and then you paste in a one-line summary and move on. But the meta description has a direct effect on your click-through rate from Google, and click-through rate affects how Google perceives the relevance of your page.</p>

<p>Getting the length right is the minimum. Getting the content right is the job.</p>

<h2>What Actually Happens When Descriptions Are Too Long</h2>

<p>Google cuts off meta descriptions in search results at roughly 155-160 characters on desktop and around 120 characters on mobile. When a description exceeds that threshold, Google truncates it with an ellipsis and the user sees an incomplete sentence. An incomplete sentence does not invite a click. It just looks sloppy.</p>

<p>More importantly, when your meta description is too long or does not contain the search query the user typed, Google will often replace it entirely with a snippet it extracts from your page content. That extracted snippet may or may not represent your page well. Writing a tight, accurate description within the character limit is your best chance of controlling what appears.</p>

<h2>What Google Does NOT Do with Meta Descriptions</h2>

<p>Meta descriptions are not a direct ranking factor. Google has confirmed this explicitly. Keywords you include in your meta description do not boost your ranking. What they do is appear in bold in the search result when they match the user's query, which draws the eye and increases click likelihood.</p>

<p>The reason descriptions matter for SEO is indirect: a well-written description increases your click-through rate, and a higher click-through rate signals to Google that your result satisfies searcher intent, which does improve ranking over time.</p>

<h2>How to Write a 155-Character Description That Works</h2>

<p>There is a reliable formula. Front-load the most important information, include the primary keyword naturally in the first half, and end with an implicit reason to click. Do not write it as a sentence fragment. Do not use clickbait phrases that set expectations the page cannot meet.</p>

<p>A good description for a calculator page might be: "Calculate your exact TDEE in seconds using the Mifflin-St Jeor formula. Free, private, and no signup required, results in your browser instantly." That is 152 characters. It includes the target keyword, explains the benefit, and addresses a common concern about sign-up friction.</p>

<h2>The 160 Character Rule Applied to Other SEO Fields</h2>

<p><strong>Title tags:</strong> The guideline is 50 to 60 characters. Google truncates longer titles in results. Put your primary keyword as close to the start as possible.</p>

<p><strong>H1 headings:</strong> No hard technical limit, but shorter headings are more scannable. Aim for under 70 characters for the main page heading.</p>

<p><strong>Alt text for images:</strong> Keep it under 125 characters. Screen readers cut off at approximately that point, which affects both accessibility and how Google interprets the image.</p>

<h2>The Most Common Meta Description Mistakes</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Writing the same description for multiple pages. Google may penalize duplicate meta descriptions across your site</li>
  <li>Leaving the description field blank. This forces Google to guess, and it rarely guesses your best selling point</li>
  <li>Stuffing keywords unnaturally, reads poorly to humans and does nothing for rankings</li>
  <li>Writing descriptions that do not match the actual page content, causes high bounce rates when users arrive and find the page does not deliver what was promised</li>
</ul>
