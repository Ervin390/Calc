<h1>Board Game Night Saved: What to Do When You Lose Your Physical Dice</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published {{ date | readableDate }} · 2 min read</p>

<p>It always happens at the worst moment. You are mid-game, turn three of Monopoly, and someone kicks over the board. The dice roll under the sofa. You find one. The other is gone.</p>

<p>Board game night does not have to end there. You have several solid options available right now without ordering replacements online or driving to a shop.</p>

<h2>Option 1: A Virtual Dice Roller (Fastest Solution)</h2>

<p>Open any browser on your phone or tablet and pull up a free online dice roller. These tools let you choose the number of sides, how many dice to roll, and even add modifiers for games that need them.</p>

<p>Pass the phone around the table. Each player rolls their turn on the same device. The results display clearly on screen and there is genuinely no difference in fairness compared to physical dice, properly built virtual rollers use cryptographic randomness from your device hardware, not cheap math shortcuts.</p>

<h2>Option 2: A Deck of Playing Cards</h2>

<p>For games that only need a standard six-sided die, a regular deck of cards works as an improvised substitute. Remove all cards above six from one suit, shuffle those six cards face down, and draw one per turn. Replace the drawn card after each draw and reshuffle occasionally.</p>

<p>It is slower than rolling, but it is completely fair and most households have a deck somewhere.</p>

<h2>Option 3: Numbered Slips from a Hat</h2>

<p>Tear a piece of paper into small equal slips, write the numbers one through six on them, fold them, and draw from a cup. Rough and ready but effective for emergencies. Kids often find this approach more fun than the dice anyway.</p>

<h2>Option 4: A Pencil Spinning Method</h2>

<p>Place a pencil flat on the table and spin it. Whichever number it points closest to on a handwritten circle divided into equal sections counts as the result. This works only for simpler games and requires a bit of setup, but it does the job if you have literally nothing else.</p>

<h2>Which Option Should You Choose?</h2>

<p>For any game that needs speed and accuracy, especially RPGs or games with multiple die types, a virtual dice roller is by far your best option. It handles d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20, and d100 with modifiers, all in one place, for free.</p>

<p>For a casual game of Ludo or Snakes and Ladders where you just need a basic d6 and do not have a phone handy, the paper and cards options work perfectly fine.</p>

<h2>Keep a Backup Handy</h2>

<p>After tonight, consider bookmarking a virtual dice roller on your phone's home screen. It takes three seconds and means you are permanently covered for every future game night regardless of what gets lost under the sofa.</p>
