<h1>Rebar Bar Size Guide: Choosing 3, 4, 5, or 6</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 4 min read</p>

<p>Steel rebar is sized using a standard US numbering system where the bar number represents its nominal diameter in eighths of an inch. For example, a #4 bar equals 4/8 of an inch, or exactly 0.5 inches in diameter.</p>

<p>Selecting the correct rebar size is essential for ensuring your concrete project meets engineering load requirements without overspending on excess steel.</p>

<h2>Common Rebar Sizes and Their Applications</h2>

<ul>
    <li><strong>#3 Rebar (3/8" / 0.375 in):</strong> Weighs 0.376 lbs/ft. Best for light residential sidewalks, driveways, wire mesh reinforcement, and garden walls.</li>
    <li><strong>#4 Rebar (1/2" / 0.500 in):</strong> Weighs 0.668 lbs/ft. The standard choice for patio slabs, garage floors, swimming pool walls, and residential driveways.</li>
    <li><strong>#5 Rebar (5/8" / 0.625 in):</strong> Weighs 1.043 lbs/ft. Used for load-bearing footings, retaining walls, and commercial slabs.</li>
    <li><strong>#6 Rebar (3/4" / 0.750 in):</strong> Weighs 1.502 lbs/ft. Designed for heavy foundations, structural columns, and highway bridge decks.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Choosing Between #3 and #4 Rebar for Home Slabs</h2>

<p>While #3 rebar works well for basic sidewalks, upgrade to #4 rebar spaced on 12-inch centers for any slab supporting vehicular traffic, such as a driveway or vehicle parking pad.</p>
