<h1>SaaS CAC Payback Period Benchmarks and Formulas</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 6 min read</p>

<p>CAC Payback Period measures the number of months required for a subscriber account to generate enough gross profit to fully recover customer acquisition costs. Payback period is the primary metric venture capitalists use to evaluate capital efficiency.</p>

<p>A shorter payback period accelerates cash recycling into sales and marketing, allowing companies to scale rapidly without dilution.</p>

<h2>The Margin-Adjusted Payback Formula</h2>

<p>To compute true cash recovery time, factor in your SaaS gross margin percentage:</p>

<div style="background: var(--card-bg, #f8fafc); border: 1px solid var(--border, #e2e8f0); padding: 1.25rem; border-radius: 8px; font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.05rem; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  CAC Payback (Months) = CAC / (ARPU x Gross Margin %)
</div>

<p>For example, if CAC is $300.00, monthly ARPU is $35.00, and gross margin is 80%:</p>

<ul>
    <li>Monthly Gross Profit: $35.00 x 0.80 = $28.00</li>
    <li>CAC Payback: $300.00 / $28.00 = <strong>10.7 months</strong></li>
</ul>

<h2>CAC Payback Benchmarks by Market Tier</h2>

<ul>
    <li><strong>SMB SaaS (Under $100 ARPU):</strong> Target payback under 12 months. High churn risks require fast payback.</li>
    <li><strong>Mid-Market SaaS ($100 to $1,000 ARPU):</strong> Target payback between 12 and 18 months.</li>
    <li><strong>Enterprise SaaS (Over $1,000 ARPU):</strong> Target payback between 18 and 24 months. Multi-year commitments offset longer payback periods.</li>
</ul>
