# How Many Working Hours in a Month: 160 vs 173

When calculating monthly salaries, hourly pay rates, or overtime figures, employers and payroll software frequently use two figures for full-time monthly hours: **160 hours** or **173.33 hours**.

Understanding the difference between 160 and 173.33 hours prevents budgeting errors, contractual disputes, and incorrect salary conversions. This guide explains how both figures are derived and when each should be applied using our [<a href="/hours-calculator/">Hours Calculator</a>](/hours-calculator/).

---

## 160 Hours: The 4-Week Month Assumption

The **160-hour figure** assumes that every month consists of exactly 4 weeks:

$$\text{Monthly Hours} = 40 \text{ hours/week} \times 4 \text{ weeks} = 160 \text{ hours}$$

### Limitation of 160 Hours:

A standard calendar year contains 52 weeks (365 days). Dividing 52 weeks by 12 months shows that a calendar month actually contains **4.333 weeks**, not 4 weeks.

If you multiply 160 hours by 12 months, you get only **1,920 hours per year**. A standard 40-hour workweek across 52 weeks equals **2,080 hours per year** ($40 \times 52 = 2,080$). Using 160 hours undercounts annual work time by 160 full hours (4 full workweeks).

---

## 173.33 Hours: The Accurate Annual Average

The **173.33-hour figure** is the official accounting standard used by software systems like QuickBooks, ADP, and Gusto:

$$\text{Annual Full-Time Hours} = 40 \text{ hours/week} \times 52 \text{ weeks} = 2,080 \text{ hours}$$

$$\text{Average Monthly Hours} = \frac{2,080 \text{ hours}}{12 \text{ months}} = 173.333 \text{ hours/month}$$

---

## Direct Comparison

| Metric | 160 Hours Method | 173.33 Hours Method |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Weekly Basis** | Assumes exactly 4 weeks/month | Assumes 4.333 weeks/month |
| **Annual Total** | 1,920 hours | 2,080 hours (Full 52 weeks) |
| **Best Used For** | 4-week short billing cycles | Standard annual salary conversion |
| **Accuracy** | 8.3% undercount annually | Exact 52-week average |

---

## Salary to Hourly Conversion Example

Consider an employee earning an annual salary of $60,000:

- **Using 173.33 Monthly Hours (Correct):**
  $$\text{Hourly Rate} = \frac{\$60,000}{2,080 \text{ hrs}} = \$28.85 \text{ / hour}$$

- **Using 160 Monthly Hours (Incorrect):**
  $$\text{Hourly Rate} = \frac{\$60,000 / 12}{160 \text{ hrs}} = \frac{\$5,000}{160} = \$31.25 \text{ / hour}$$
