<h2>1. Preventing Content Distraction During UI Reviews</h2>

<p>The primary reason designers use dummy text is cognitive psychology. Human brains are hardwired to read and process readable language automatically. If a client or stakeholder reviews a website mockup filled with real English copy, they immediately start editing the wording, tweaking product headlines, or debating grammar points.</p>

<p>By filling wireframes with nonsensical text that has a natural letter distribution, clients focus entirely on visual elements: typographic hierarchy, spacing, grid layout, color contrast, and component placement.</p>

<h2>2. Natural Word Length and Letter Frequency</h2>

<p>Why not simply repeat the phrase <em>"Content goes here content goes here"</em> across the page? Simple repeated phrases create artificial visual patterns. English words vary dramatically in length, containing a natural blend of short prepositions and long multisyllabic nouns.</p>

<p>Lorem Ipsum mimics standard Latin-script languages with realistic word length variance and letter spacing, creating an authentic visual texture without readable meaning.</p>

<h2>3. Where Did Lorem Ipsum Originate?</h2>

<p>Contrary to popular belief, Lorem Ipsum is not random gibberish. It is rooted in classical Latin literature dating back to 45 BC. In the 1960s, Latin scholar Richard McClintock discovered that the passage comes from sections 1.10.32 and 1.10.33 of Marcus Tullius Cicero's famous philosophical treatise <em>De finibus bonorum et malorum</em> (On the Ends of Good and Evil).</p>

<p>The original phrase was <em>"Neque porro quisquam est qui dolorem ipsum quia dolor sit amet, consectetur, adipisci velit"</em>, which translates to: <em>"Neither is there anyone who loves or pursues or desires to obtain pain of itself, because it is pain."</em> Typesetters in the 16th century scrambled parts of Cicero's manuscript to create a specimen page book.</p>
