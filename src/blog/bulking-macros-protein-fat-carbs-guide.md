<h1>How to Calculate the Perfect Grams of Protein, Fat, and Carbs for Muscle Bulking</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published {{ date | readableDate }} · 2 min read</p>

<p>Bulking is simple in principle. Eat more than you burn, train progressively, recover adequately, and your body builds muscle. The complications arrive when you try to do it efficiently, gains without burying yourself in unnecessary fat that takes months to cut.</p>

<p>Here is how to set your macros for a genuine lean bulk.</p>

<h2>Step 1: Establish Your TDEE First</h2>

<p>You cannot set a useful surplus without knowing your maintenance calories. Your TDEE is the baseline, the total daily energy your body burns including activity. Start from that number and add 200 to 300 calories above it for a conservative lean bulk that minimizes fat gain while still providing enough energy for muscle synthesis.</p>

<p>More aggressive surpluses of 500 calories above TDEE build mass faster but typically result in a higher ratio of fat to muscle in the gained weight. Unless you are significantly below your genetic muscle ceiling, a smaller surplus is usually more efficient. </p>

<h2>Step 2: Set Your Protein Target</h2>

<p>For muscle building, the widely supported range is 0.7 to 1 gram of protein per pound of bodyweight (roughly 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilogram). At this range, protein synthesis is effectively maximized for most people.</p>

<p>A 180-pound (82 kg) person should target approximately 130 to 180 grams of protein per day. Each gram of protein provides 4 calories, so 160 grams of protein = 640 calories from protein.</p>

<h2>Step 3: Set Fat and Carbohydrates</h2>

<p>Fat is essential for hormone production including testosterone, which directly affects muscle synthesis. A floor of 0.4 grams per pound of bodyweight (or 20 to 25 percent of total calories from fat) maintains healthy hormone function.</p>

<p>Carbohydrates fill the remaining calories. They are the primary fuel for intense training directly. During a bulk, carbohydrates should make up the largest share of your remaining calories after protein and fat minimums are met.</p>

<h2>Tracking Progress and Adjusting</h2>

<p>Weigh yourself once per week at the same time of day. If you are gaining more than 0.5 to 1 percent of bodyweight per month, you are likely gaining more fat than necessary. Reduce calories by 100 and reassess. If you are not gaining at all after 3 weeks at your target intake, increase by 100 calories.</p>

<p>Body composition takes months to shift meaningfully. Commit to a full 12 to 16 week bulk before evaluating whether the approach is working.</p>
