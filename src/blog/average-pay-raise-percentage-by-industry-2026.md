<h1>Average Pay Raise Percentage by Industry in 2026</h1>
<p style="color:#6b7280; font-size:0.9rem;">Published August 9, 2026 · 5 min read</p>

<p>Before you walk into a raise conversation, you need to know what the market is actually paying. Getting a 2% raise feels great if your industry average is 1.5%, and terrible if it is 6%. Context is everything.</p>

<p>Here is a breakdown of what organizations across major sectors are budgeting for raises in 2026, based on compensation surveys from WorldatWork, Mercer, and Willis Towers Watson.</p>

<h2>Overall Market Baseline</h2>

<p>The median salary budget increase across all US industries in 2026 sits at approximately 3.7%. That is slightly higher than the pre-pandemic average of 3% but has pulled back from the elevated 4% to 4.5% range seen during the 2021 to 2023 labor shortage period. Inflation cooling has given employers a bit more breathing room, and the urgency to retain workers through large increases has softened somewhat.</p>

<h2>What These Numbers Mean for Your Negotiation</h2>

<p>A raise at or below the industry average is essentially the maintenance raise. It keeps you even with market movement but does not close any gap that may have opened. If inflation runs at 3% and your raise is 3.5%, your real purchasing power increases by maybe half a percent.</p>

<p>Top performers typically receive 1.5 to 2 times the median raise. If your company's budget is 3.7% and you receive 3.7%, you are performing at median expectations. If the budget is 3.7% and you receive 5.5%, you are in the high-performer category.</p>

<h2>When to Negotiate</h2>

<p>The best window is three to four months before your formal review. That is when budgets are being built. Walking in on review day asking for more puts you in a much weaker position because the budget allocations are usually already set. Making your case early, with specific examples of your contributions, gives managers time to advocate for a larger number on your behalf.</p>

<p>Changing jobs remains the fastest path to a meaningful salary jump. Internal raises typically max out at 10% even for strong performers. Competing offers from outside sometimes unlock 15% to 25% increases for the same or similar work.</p>
