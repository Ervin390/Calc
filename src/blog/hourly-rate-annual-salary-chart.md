<h2>Why Monthly Pay Does Not Equal Weekly Pay Multiplied by Four</h2>

<p>Many folks make the mistake of multiplying weekly pay by four to estimate monthly earnings. A week has seven days, and a year has 52 weeks. If you multiply 52 by seven, you get 364 days, leaving one leftover day in normal years and two in leap years. That is why months have slightly more than four weeks.</p>

<p>To find the true monthly average, divide the annual salary by 12. Multiplying weekly pay by 4.33 gives you the same result. The difference is worth noting when budgeting for recurring expenses like rent or car loans.</p>

<h2>Factoring in Unpaid Time Off</h2>

<p>Most hourly roles do not include paid time off. If you take two weeks of unpaid vacation, you only get paid for 50 weeks of work, cutting your annual hours to 2,000.</p>

<p>A $25 hourly rate with two weeks off drops to $50,000 per year, compared to the $52,000 standard figure. Keep this in mind when comparing salary offers that include paid vacation to contracting rates that do not.</p>
