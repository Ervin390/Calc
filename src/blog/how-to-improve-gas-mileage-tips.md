<h1>How to Improve Gas Mileage: Practical Tips That Actually Work</h1>
<p style="color:#6b7280; font-size:0.9rem;">Published August 9, 2026 · 5 min read</p>

<p>Every time gas prices spike everyone starts looking for ways to burn less fuel. Some of the advice floating around the internet is useless, like turning off your air conditioning in the dead of summer to save a few pennies. But there are several practical changes that make a measurable difference in your monthly fuel budget.</p>

<p>Most of these adjustments cost absolutely nothing to implement because they focus on driving habits and basic vehicle care.</p>

<h2>Keep Your Driving Speed Steady</h2>

<p>The single most effective way to improve your MPG is to slow down and drive smoothly. Aggressive driving with rapid acceleration and hard braking wastes an enormous amount of fuel. The Department of Energy estimates that aggressive behavior lowers your gas mileage by 15% to 30% at highway speeds.</p>

<p>Vehicles are engineered to be most efficient at different speeds, but for almost all cars that sweet spot is between 50 and 60 miles per hour. Once you pass 60, aerodynamic drag increases exponentially. Driving at 75 miles per hour burns significantly more fuel than driving at 65, even though you reach your destination only a few minutes faster.</p>

<h2>Check Your Tire Pressure Once a Month</h2>

<p>Under-inflated tires increase rolling resistance. Think about how much harder it is to pedal a bicycle with soft tires. The same physics applies to your car engine pulling the weight of the vehicle.</p>

<p>For every 1 PSI drop in pressure across all four tires, your fuel economy decreases by roughly 0.2%. Check the recommended PSI on the sticker inside your driver's door storage pillar rather than the maximum pressure listed on the tire itself. Keep a cheap pressure gauge in your glove box and inspect them when the tires are cold.</p>

<h2>Lighten the Load in Your Trunk</h2>

<p>Extra weight demands more energy to move. Carrying an extra 100 pounds in your vehicle reduces your fuel economy by about 1%. The effect is even more pronounced in smaller, lighter cars.</p>

<p>Clear out heavy sports gear, bags of sand, or unnecessary tools from your trunk. Roof racks and cargo boxes also drag down efficiency by creating massive wind resistance. Remove roof accessories when you are not actively using them for a road trip.</p>

<h2>Avoid Excessive Idling</h2>

<p>Idling gets you zero miles per gallon. Modern fuel-injected engines only need about 30 seconds to warm up even on freezing mornings. If you are waiting for someone or parked at a long train crossing, turn off the engine. Letting it run for more than ten seconds uses more fuel than restarting the engine would.</p>

<p>Following these four habits can easily save you $20 to $50 per month, keeping more of your hard-earned wages in your wallet.</p>
