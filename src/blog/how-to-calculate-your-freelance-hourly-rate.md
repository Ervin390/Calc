<h1>How to Calculate Your Freelance Hourly Rate</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 6 min read</p>

<p>Most freelancers start out by guessing their hourly price or copying a competitor's rates. That approach often leads to working 50 hours a week while earning less than an entry-level salaried job. When you work for yourself, your rate must cover personal income target, self-employment taxes, insurance, software tools, and unpaid administrative time.</p>

<p>Calculating a sustainable freelance rate requires starting with your desired net income and working backward to account for real business expenses.</p>

<h2>Step 1: Determine Total Gross Revenue Needed</h2>

<p>Add your desired personal take-home pay, annual business overhead (software, hardware, hosting, accounting), and self-employment tax buffer (15.3% in the US).</p>

<p>For example, suppose you want a net personal income of $75,000. Your software subscriptions and equipment cost $5,000 per year. Adding taxes and expenses means your business must generate roughly $95,000 in total gross revenue.</p>

<h2>Step 2: Calculate True Billable Hours</h2>

<p>You cannot bill 40 hours a week for 52 weeks. Administrative tasks like sending proposals, invoicing clients, updating your portfolio, and answering emails take up significant time. Most full-time freelancers average 25 to 30 billable hours per week.</p>

<ul>
    <li>52 weeks minus 4 weeks off (vacation/sick) = 48 working weeks</li>
    <li>48 weeks x 40 total hours = 1,920 working hours</li>
    <li>If 30% of time is non-billable admin, billable ratio is 70%</li>
    <li>True annual billable hours: 1,920 x 0.70 = <strong>1,344 hours</strong></li>
</ul>

<h2>Step 3: Calculate Your Minimum Hourly Rate</h2>

<p>Divide your required gross revenue ($95,000) by your annual billable hours (1,344). Your minimum target rate comes out to <strong>$70.68 per hour</strong>.</p>
