<h1>Flat vs Tiered Commission Structures Explained</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 4 min read</p>

<p>Choosing the right incentive plan is critical when designing compensation models for sales organizations. The two most prominent approaches are flat commission rates and tiered accelerator plans.</p>

<p>Both models reward closed revenue, but they distribute payouts differently based on performance volume.</p>

<h2>Flat Commission Plans</h2>

<p>A flat commission plan pays a fixed percentage on every dollar of revenue generated. For instance, a 7% flat rate pays $3,500 on $50,000 in sales and $7,000 on $100,000 in sales.</p>

<p>Flat plans offer simplicity and predictability. Sales reps always know their payout ratio, making income forecasting straightforward.</p>

<h2>Tiered Commission Plans</h2>

<p>Tiered plans scale the payout percentage upward as reps pass specific quota targets. A representative might earn 5% up to quota, 8% after hitting quota, and 12% for super-achievers.</p>

<p>Side-by-side comparison of total earnings on $150,000 in deals:</p>

<ul>
    <li><strong>Flat 7% Rate:</strong> $150,000 x 0.07 = <strong>$10,500.00</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Tiered (5% on $50k / 8% on next $50k / 12% on remaining $50k):</strong> $2,500 + $4,000 + $6,000 = <strong>$12,500.00</strong></li>
</ul>

<h2>Which Model Works Best?</h2>

<p>Flat plans suit transactional sales cycles with consistent deal sizes. Tiered plans excel in competitive B2B sales where motivating reps to stretch beyond 100% quota yields major growth benefits.</p>
