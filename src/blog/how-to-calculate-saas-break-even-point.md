<h1>How to Calculate SaaS Break Even Point</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 6 min read</p>

<p>Reaching break-even is a critical milestone for any subscription software startup. Breaking even marks the pivot point where gross margin profit fully covers fixed operating overhead, eliminating ongoing cash burn.</p>

<p>Calculating SaaS break-even requires evaluating fixed monthly expenses against net margin profit contribution per active subscriber account.</p>

<div style="background:var(--input-bg); border-left: 4px solid var(--primary); padding: 1.25rem; border-radius: 6px; margin: 2rem 0;">

<h2>The SaaS Break-Even Account Formula</h2>

<p>To determine how many paying subscribers are needed to reach net operating break-even:</p>

<div style="background: var(--card-bg, #f8fafc); border: 1px solid var(--border, #e2e8f0); padding: 1.25rem; border-radius: 8px; font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.05rem; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  Break-Even Subscribers = Monthly Fixed Overhead / (ARPU x Gross Margin %)
</div>

<p>Let us walk through a typical startup scenario:</p>

<ul>
    <li>Monthly Fixed Overhead (Payroll, Rent, Software): $15,000.00</li>
    <li>Average Monthly Revenue Per User (ARPU): $50.00</li>
    <li>SaaS Gross Margin: 80% (0.80)</li>
    <li>Net Margin Profit / User: $50.00 x 0.80 = $40.00</li>
    <li>Break-Even Target: $15,000 / $40.00 = <strong>375 Active Accounts</strong></li>
</ul>

<h2>Calculating Break-Even Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)</h2>

<p>To convert subscriber count into required monthly recurring revenue:</p>

<p><code>Break-Even MRR = Break-Even Accounts x ARPU</code></p>

<p>With 375 accounts at $50 ARPU: <code>375 x $50 = $18,750.00 MRR</code>.</p>
