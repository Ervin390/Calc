<h1>ACV vs ARR Explained for Enterprise Software</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 5 min read</p>

<p>When reporting financial metrics to board members and venture capital investors, SaaS operators must distinguish between Annual Contract Value (ACV) and Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR).</p>

<p>While ARR measures total company-wide recurring revenue, ACV measures the average annualized revenue generated by a single customer contract.</p>

<h2>Defining Annual Contract Value (ACV)</h2>

<p>ACV calculates the average annual revenue value of a single customer subscription contract across its active term:</p>

<div style="background: var(--card-bg, #f8fafc); border: 1px solid var(--border, #e2e8f0); padding: 1.25rem; border-radius: 8px; font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.05rem; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  ACV = Total ARR / Number of Active Customer Accounts
</div>

<p>For example, if a SaaS company generates $300,000.00 in total ARR across 50 active customer accounts, your ACV is <code>$300,000 / 50 = $6,000.00 per account</code>.</p>

<h2>Comparing ACV and ARR Side by Side</h2>

<ul>
    <li><strong>Scope:</strong> ARR measures macro company revenue; ACV measures micro contract size.</li>
    <li><strong>Sales Strategy:</strong> High ACV ($50,000+) supports outbound enterprise sales teams; low ACV ($1,000) requires self-serve product-led growth.</li>
</ul>
