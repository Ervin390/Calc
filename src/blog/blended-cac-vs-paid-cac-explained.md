<h1>Blended CAC vs Paid CAC Explained for SaaS</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 5 min read</p>

<p>When measuring acquisition efficiency, software founders often conflate Paid CAC with Blended CAC. While both metrics measure customer acquisition costs, analyzing them independently provides critical insights into channel performance and operational scale.</p>

<p>Relying solely on Paid CAC creates a false sense of security by ignoring sales team compensation, content marketing overhead, and software subscriptions.</p>

<h2>Defining Paid CAC</h2>

<p>Paid CAC measures the direct campaign spend required to acquire a customer exclusively through paid advertising channels such as Google Search, Meta Ads, and LinkedIn Ads:</p>

<div style="background: var(--card-bg, #f8fafc); border: 1px solid var(--border, #e2e8f0); padding: 1.25rem; border-radius: 8px; font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.05rem; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  Paid CAC = Ad Campaign Spend / Customers Acquired via Paid Channels
</div>

<p>For example, if you spend $10,000 on Google Search Ads and acquire 100 paid conversions, your Paid CAC is <strong>$100.00</strong> per customer.</p>

<h2>Defining Blended CAC</h2>

<p>Blended CAC includes all sales and marketing costs across paid, organic, referral, and partner channels divided by total new customers acquired:</p>

<div style="background: var(--card-bg, #f8fafc); border: 1px solid var(--border, #e2e8f0); padding: 1.25rem; border-radius: 8px; font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.05rem; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  Blended CAC = Total S&M Spend (Ad Spend + Salaries + Overhead) / Total New Customers
</div>

<p>If overall sales and marketing spend is $30,000 and total acquired customers across organic and paid channels is 100, your Blended CAC is <strong>$300.00</strong> per customer.</p>

<h2>When to Use Each Metric</h2>

<ul>
    <li><strong>Use Paid CAC:</strong> To optimize paid ad budgets, bid targets, and landing page conversion rates per channel.</li>
    <li><strong>Use Blended CAC:</strong> For board reporting, venture capital fundraising, and long-term financial modeling.</li>
</ul>
