<h1>How to Calculate Prorated Salary Mid-Month</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 5 min read</p>

<p>When you start a new salaried position or leave a job in the middle of a pay period, your paycheck is prorated. Employers calculate a partial salary payment to cover the exact workdays you were on payroll rather than paying a full monthly pay period.</p>

<p>While salaried roles offer predictable annual compensation, mid-month transitions require daily rate accounting.</p>

<h2>The Working Days Method (Standard HR Formula)</h2>

<p>Most HR software systems divide your annual salary by 260 standard workdays (52 weeks x 5 days) to determine your daily salary rate. You are then paid for the actual workdays completed during that partial pay period.</p>

<p>Let us walk through a practical example:</p>

<ul>
    <li>Annual base salary: $78,000.00</li>
    <li>New job start date: April 13th</li>
    <li>Total workdays in April: 22 workdays</li>
    <li>Actual workdays completed: 14 workdays</li>
</ul>

<p>First, determine the daily rate: $78,000 divided by 260 workdays equals <strong>$300.00 per day</strong>. Next, multiply $300.00 by the 14 days worked. Your prorated gross pay comes to <strong>$4,200.00</strong>.</p>

<h2>The Calendar Days Alternative</h2>

<p>Some international payroll systems divide annual salary by 365 calendar days. Under that method, the daily rate on a $78,000 salary is $213.70 per day, multiplied by total calendar days employed during the month.</p>
