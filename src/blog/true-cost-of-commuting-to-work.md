<h1>The True Cost of Commuting to Work Each Month</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 6 min read</p>

<p>When evaluating a job offer or calculating monthly expenses, most workers only count the gas money spent at the pump. That focus ignores vehicle depreciation, maintenance wear, highway tolls, and parking fees, which together double or triple your true out-of-pocket transportation costs.</p>

<p>Calculating your real commute expenses reveals how much money you actually spend traveling to an office.</p>

<h2>Worked Example of a Daily 25-Mile Commute</h2>

<p>Consider an employee with a 25-mile one-way commute (50 miles round trip), driving 5 days a week in a gasoline vehicle averaging 30 MPG. Gas costs $3.50 per gallon, tolls cost $4.00 per day, and monthly office parking is $100.00.</p>

<ol>
    <li><strong>Annual Miles:</strong> 50 miles x 5 days x 52 weeks = 13,000 miles/year</li>
    <li><strong>Annual Fuel Cost:</strong> (13,000 / 30) x $3.50 = <strong>$1,516.67</strong></li>
    <li><strong>IRS Mileage Wear & Depreciation ($0.67/mi):</strong> 13,000 x $0.67 = <strong>$8,710.00</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Tolls & Parking:</strong> ($4.00 x 260 days) + ($100 x 12) = <strong>$2,240.00</strong></li>
</ol>

<p>While annual fuel costs seem manageable at $1,516.67, the total true cost of this commute reaching 13,000 miles per year comes out to <strong>$12,466.67</strong> annually (over $1,000 per month).</p>

<h2>What Salary Raise Equals Working Remote?</h2>

<p>In this scenario, taking a remote position saves over $12,000 in gross after-tax cash. To match that financial gain at an office job, you would need an additional salary raise of over $15,000 before income taxes.</p>
