# Mifflin-St Jeor Equation Step by Step Guide

The **Mifflin-St Jeor Equation** is universally considered by dietitians and medical professionals to be the most accurate formula for estimating a human's **Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)**-the number of calories burned while at complete rest.

Developed in 1990 by MD Mifflin and ST St Jeor, this formula improved upon older equations (like the Harris-Benedict equation) by accounting for modern population body composition.

This guide details the step-by-step mathematical calculations for men and women using our [<a href="/bmr-calculator/">BMR Calculator</a>](/bmr-calculator/).

---

## The Mifflin-St Jeor Equations

Variables required:
- $W$ = Weight in kilograms ($\text{lbs} \div 2.2046$)
- $H$ = Height in centimeters ($\text{inches} \times 2.54$)
- $A$ = Age in years

### Formula for Men:
$$\text{BMR} = (10 \times W) + (6.25 \times H) - (5 \times A) + 5$$

### Formula for Women:
$$\text{BMR} = (10 \times W) + (6.25 \times H) - (5 \times A) - 161$$

---

## Worked Calculation Example (Female)

Calculate BMR for a 35-year-old woman weighing **65 kg** (143 lbs) with a height of **165 cm** (5 ft 5 in):

### Step 1: Multiply Weight ($10 \times W$)
$$10 \times 65 = 650$$

### Step 2: Multiply Height ($6.25 \times H$)
$$6.25 \times 165 = 1031.25$$

### Step 3: Multiply Age ($5 \times A$)
$$5 \times 35 = 175$$

### Step 4: Apply Formula with Female Constant (-161)
$$\text{BMR} = 650 + 1031.25 - 175 - 161 = \mathbf{1,345.25 \text{ kcal / day}}$$

At complete bed rest, her body requires approximately **1,345 calories per day** to sustain vital organ functions.

---

## BMR vs Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)

BMR does not account for physical activity. To calculate total daily calorie needs (TDEE), multiply BMR by an activity factor:
- **Sedentary (Desk Job):** $\text{BMR} \times 1.2$
- **Lightly Active (1-3 days/wk exercise):** $\text{BMR} \times 1.375$
- **Moderately Active (3-5 days/wk exercise):** $\text{BMR} \times 1.55$
