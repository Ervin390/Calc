<h1>How to Calculate Monthly Recurring Revenue in SaaS</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 6 min read</p>

<p>Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) is the primary operating metric for subscription software companies. MRR normalizes varying billing cycles and contract lengths into a predictable monthly figure, enabling accurate financial modeling.</p>

<p>Tracking clean MRR requires isolating recurring subscription fees from one-time setup charges, professional consulting retainers, and hardware fees.</p>

<h2>The Core MRR Formula</h2>

<p>Basic Monthly Recurring Revenue is computed by multiplying active paying subscribers by your average revenue per user and adding recurring add-on charges:</p>

<div style="background: var(--card-bg, #f8fafc); border: 1px solid var(--border, #e2e8f0); padding: 1.25rem; border-radius: 8px; font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.05rem; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  MRR = (Active Subscribers x Base ARPU) + Monthly Recurring Add-ons
</div>

<p>Let us review an example calculation:</p>

<ul>
    <li>Active Subscribers: 250</li>
    <li>Base Tier ARPU: $49.00</li>
    <li>Base Recurring Revenue: 250 x $49.00 = $12,250.00</li>
    <li>Monthly Add-on & Seat Revenue: $1,250.00</li>
    <li>Total MRR: $12,250 + $1,250 = <strong>$13,500.00 MRR</strong></li>
</ul>

<h2>Converting MRR to Annual Run Rate (ARR)</h2>

<p>To project your annual recurring revenue run rate based on current monthly performance:</p>

<p><code>ARR = MRR x 12</code></p>

<p>With $13,500 MRR, your annualized run rate is <code>$13,500 x 12 = $162,000.00 ARR</code>.</p>
