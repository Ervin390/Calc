<h1>How to Calculate Paint Coverage Per Gallon & Liter</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 17, 2026 · 5 min read</p>

<p>When shopping for interior or exterior paint, every paint can label displays a spreading rate statement such as covers 350 to 400 square feet per gallon. Understanding how paint spreading rates translate to actual room surfaces helps homeowners avoid overspending on unneeded paint cans.</p>

<p>Paint spreading rates depend on surface porosity, application methods, and paint quality. Smooth drywall uses significantly less paint than porous concrete block or rough wood siding.</p>

<h2>How Wall Texture Impacts Paint Spreading Rates</h2>

<p>Surface texture acts like a sponge for liquid paint. While smooth primed drywall achieves maximum 400 sq ft per gallon coverage, other substrates require extra paint volume:</p>

<ul>
    <li><strong>Unprimed Drywall:</strong> Soaks up first coats quickly. Reduces coverage to 250 sq ft per gallon. Applying a dedicated primer seal coat first is much cheaper than using premium paint as a primer.</li>
    <li><strong>Textured Plaster & Stucco:</strong> Rough orange peel or knockdown textures increase surface area by up to 25 percent. Expect 275 sq ft per gallon coverage.</li>
    <li><strong>Masonry & Brick:</strong> Highly porous surface. Expect 200 to 250 sq ft per gallon coverage.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Converting Gallons to Liters for International Projects</h2>

<p>One US gallon equals 3.785 liters. In metric markets, paint coverage is measured in square meters per liter. One liter of standard interior wall paint covers approximately 9 square meters per coat.</p>
