<h1>How to Make Your Text Bold or Italic on Instagram and TikTok Bios</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published {{ date | readableDate }} · 2 min read</p>

<p>Instagram does not have a bold button. Neither does TikTok. But plenty of accounts you follow have bold text, italic text, or stylized fonts in their bios right now. How are they doing it?</p>

<p>The answer is Unicode. Every character you see on your screen has a code point in the Unicode standard, and Unicode includes complete alternative alphabets that look bold, italic, script, and monospace. These are not fonts applied on top of standard letters. They are genuinely different characters that display consistently across every device and platform.</p>

<h2>Why This Works Everywhere</h2>

<p>When you type a regular "A" you are entering the character at Unicode position U+0041. A bold mathematical "A" lives at U+1D400. They look different but they are both just characters. Instagram, TikTok, X, LinkedIn, and most other platforms accept text input, and that includes these alternative Unicode characters. None of them are blocked because they are not tricks or exploits. They are legitimate characters the Unicode Consortium officially standardized.</p>

<h2>Best Styles for Each Platform</h2>

<p><strong>Instagram bio:</strong> Bold and script styles tend to work best. They are readable at small sizes and give a premium feel without looking like you are trying too hard. Avoid styles with heavy diacritics or strikethrough. Those read as messy on mobile screens.</p>

<p><strong>TikTok username:</strong> Short usernames work well in monospace or double-struck styles. The squatter character width of monospace makes short names feel more substantial.</p>

<p><strong>X (Twitter):</strong> Bold text in tweets creates natural visual hierarchy and draws the eye to key points. Useful for announcements or thread openers where you want the first line to stand out from the replies below.</p>

<p><strong>LinkedIn headline:</strong> Script fonts carry an elegant professional weight. Use them sparingly, one stylized word in an otherwise normal headline is more effective than styling the entire line.</p>

<h2>What Platform Restrictions Exist?</h2>

<p>Most platforms that accept Unicode text display these characters without issue. The main limitations are:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Platform search does not always index Unicode characters correctly, so using a stylized name can make you harder to find through search</li>
  <li>Screen readers used by visually impaired users read out the technical Unicode character name rather than the intended letter, which can create poor accessibility</li>
  <li>Some characters do not render consistently on older Android devices</li>
</ul>

<p>For bios and usernames these trade-offs are usually acceptable. For main post content, standard text is always the safer accessibility-conscious choice.</p>
