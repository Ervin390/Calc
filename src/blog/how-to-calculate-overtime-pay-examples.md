<h1>How to Calculate Overtime Pay: Step-by-Step Examples</h1>
<p style="color:#6b7280; font-size:0.9rem;">Published August 9, 2026 · 8 min read</p>

<p>Calculating overtime pay should be simple and transparent. Whether you are reviewing your weekly pay stubs, budgeting for extra shifts, or managing payroll for a growing business, knowing how overtime math works protects you from payroll errors and ensures accurate compensation.</p>

<p>This comprehensive guide breaks down six real-world overtime calculation scenarios step by step, covering regular hourly wages, bi-weekly schedules, salaried non-exempt compensation, California daily overtime rules, shift differentials, and blended rates for multi-role positions.</p>

<h2>The General Overtime Math Formula</h2>

<p>Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), non-exempt employees in the United States earn time and a half (1.5 times their regular hourly rate) for all hours worked beyond 40 hours in a single 7-day workweek.</p>

<ul>
    <li><code>Overtime Rate = Regular Hourly Rate x 1.5</code></li>
    <li><code>Regular Pay = Base Hours (up to 40) x Regular Hourly Rate</code></li>
    <li><code>Overtime Pay = Overtime Hours x Overtime Rate</code></li>
    <li><code>Total Gross Pay = Regular Pay + Overtime Pay</code></li>
</ul>

<h2>Scenario 1: Standard Hourly Worker</h2>

<p>Maria works as a logistics associate earning $18.50 per hour. Due to a seasonal surge in warehouse shipments, she clocks 47 hours during a single 7-day workweek.</p>

<h3>Step-by-Step Calculation:</h3>
<ol>
    <li><strong>Calculate Regular Pay:</strong> 40 regular hours x $18.50 = <strong>$740.00</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Calculate Overtime Rate:</strong> $18.50 x 1.5 = <strong>$27.75 per hour</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Calculate Overtime Pay:</strong> 7 overtime hours x $27.75 = <strong>$194.25</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Calculate Total Gross Pay:</strong> $740.00 + $194.25 = <strong>$934.25</strong></li>
</ol>

<p>Because Maria worked 7 hours of overtime, her gross paycheck increased by $194.25 compared to a standard 40-hour week ($740.00 base pay).</p>

<h2>Scenario 2: Bi-Weekly Pay Schedule (Weekly Overtime Tracking)</h2>

<p>A common payroll misunderstanding occurs when employees receive pay bi-weekly (every two weeks). Federal law requires overtime to be calculated strictly per individual workweek, not averaged across the two-week pay period.</p>

<p>Consider David, a healthcare assistant earning $22.00 per hour, who receives pay every two weeks:</p>
<ul>
    <li><strong>Week 1 Hours:</strong> 50 hours (10 hours overtime)</li>
    <li><strong>Week 2 Hours:</strong> 30 hours (0 hours overtime)</li>
    <li><strong>Total Bi-Weekly Hours:</strong> 80 hours</li>
</ul>

<h3>Step-by-Step Calculation:</h3>
<ol>
    <li><strong>Week 1 Calculation:</strong> 40 regular hours x $22.00 ($880.00) + 10 overtime hours x $33.00 ($330.00) = <strong>$1,210.00</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Week 2 Calculation:</strong> 30 regular hours x $22.00 = <strong>$660.00</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Bi-Weekly Gross Total:</strong> $1,210.00 + $660.00 = <strong>$1,870.00</strong></li>
</ol>

<p><strong>Crucial Note:</strong> If an employer incorrectly averaged the 80 total hours as 40 hours per week, David would receive $1,760.00 (80 x $22.00). Averaging across workweeks deprives David of $110.00 in earned overtime compensation and constitutes an FLSA violation.</p>

<h2>Scenario 3: Salaried Non-Exempt Employee</h2>

<p>James earns an annual fixed salary of $41,600.00 ($800.00 per week for an expected 40-hour schedule) as an administrative coordinator. Because his weekly earnings fall below federal exemption thresholds or his duties are non-managerial, he remains legally classified as non-exempt.</p>

<p>During a week preparing for an annual corporate audit, James works 48 hours.</p>

<h3>Step-by-Step Calculation:</h3>
<ol>
    <li><strong>Determine Regular Hourly Equivalent:</strong> $800.00 weekly salary / 40 base hours = <strong>$20.00 per hour</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Determine Overtime Rate:</strong> $20.00 x 1.5 = <strong>$30.00 per hour</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Calculate Overtime Pay:</strong> 8 overtime hours x $30.00 = <strong>$240.00</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Calculate Total Pay for the Audit Week:</strong> $800.00 base salary + $240.00 overtime = <strong>$1,040.00</strong></li>
</ol>

<h2>Scenario 4: California Daily Overtime & Double Time</h2>

<p>California labor law imposes daily overtime thresholds in addition to federal weekly rules. Employees in California earn:</p>
<ul>
    <li>1.5x regular rate for hours worked beyond 8 hours in a single workday (up to 12 hours).</li>
    <li>2.0x regular rate (Double Time) for hours worked beyond 12 hours in a single workday, or past 8 hours on the 7th consecutive day of a workweek.</li>
</ul>

<p>Sarah, an IT system engineer in California earning $30.00 per hour, works a 14-hour emergency server recovery shift in a single day.</p>

<h3>Step-by-Step Shift Calculation:</h3>
<ol>
    <li><strong>Base Hours (1 to 8):</strong> 8 hours x $30.00 = <strong>$240.00</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Overtime Hours (9 to 12):</strong> 4 hours x ($30.00 x 1.5 = $45.00) = <strong>$180.00</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Double Time Hours (13 to 14):</strong> 2 hours x ($30.00 x 2.0 = $60.00) = <strong>$120.00</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Total Shift Gross Pay:</strong> $240.00 + $180.00 + $120.00 = <strong>$540.00</strong></li>
</ol>

<h2>Scenario 5: Blended Rate for Multiple Roles with One Employer</h2>

<p>When an employee works two different jobs with different pay rates for the same employer during one workweek, FLSA rules mandate calculating overtime using a <strong>blended regular rate</strong> based on total weighted earnings.</p>

<p>Marcus works at a hotel. In a single week, he works:</p>
<ul>
    <li>30 hours as a Front Desk Agent at $16.00 per hour ($480.00)</li>
    <li>15 hours as a Shift Supervisor at $20.00 per hour ($300.00)</li>
    <li>Total Hours Worked: 45 hours (5 overtime hours)</li>
</ul>

<h3>Step-by-Step Calculation:</h3>
<ol>
    <li><strong>Calculate Total Base Straight-Time Pay:</strong> $480.00 + $300.00 = <strong>$780.00</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Calculate Blended Regular Hourly Rate:</strong> $780.00 total base / 45 total hours = <strong>$17.33 per hour</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Calculate Overtime Half-Time Premium:</strong> $17.33 x 0.5 = <strong>$8.67 per hour</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Calculate Additional Overtime Pay:</strong> 5 overtime hours x $8.67 = <strong>$43.35</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Calculate Total Gross Compensation:</strong> $780.00 + $43.35 = <strong>$823.35</strong></li>
</ol>
