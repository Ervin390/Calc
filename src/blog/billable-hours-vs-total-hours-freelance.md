<h1>Billable Hours vs Total Hours for Freelancers</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 4 min read</p>

<p>The biggest trap for new freelancers is assuming every hour at their desk can be billed to a client. In reality, running an independent business involves significant unbilled overhead, from prospecting new leads to updating project management software.</p>

<p>Failing to distinguish between billable hours and total hours leads directly to burnout and income shortfalls.</p>

<h2>What Counts as Billable Time?</h2>

<p>Billable hours represent time spent directly producing client deliverables. Examples include writing code, designing graphics, editing copy, participating in scheduled client strategy calls, or conducting requested research.</p>

<h2>What Counts as Non-Billable Overhead?</h2>

<p>Non-billable hours are business activities necessary to keep your practice operating. They include:</p>

<ul>
    <li>Writing sales proposals and scope-of-work documents</li>
    <li>Sending invoices and tracking down client payments</li>
    <li>Bookkeeping, tax preparation, and receipts organizing</li>
    <li>Website maintenance and portfolio updates</li>
    <li>Continuing education and professional development</li>
</ul>

<h2>Industry Standards for Billable Ratios</h2>

<p>For most solopreneurs, a sustainable billable ratio sits between 60% and 75%. If you work 40 total hours per week, expect roughly 25 to 30 of those hours to generate direct revenue.</p>
