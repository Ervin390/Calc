<h1>How to Calculate Customer Lifetime Value in SaaS</h1>
<p class="post-meta">Published August 24, 2026 · 6 min read</p>

<p>Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) is the foundational metric for evaluating unit economics in subscription software. LTV measures the total revenue or gross profit a single customer generates across their entire relationship with your product before cancelling.</p>

<p>Without an accurate LTV calculation, software executives risk overspending on customer acquisition or under-investing in high-value enterprise sales channels.</p>

<h2>The Core Formula for Gross SaaS LTV</h2>

<p>At its simplest, gross customer lifetime value equals your Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) divided by your monthly account churn rate:</p>

<div style="background: var(--card-bg, #f8fafc); border: 1px solid var(--border, #e2e8f0); padding: 1.25rem; border-radius: 8px; font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.05rem; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  Gross LTV = ARPU / Monthly Churn Rate
</div>

<p>Let us walk through a practical example:</p>

<ul>
    <li>Monthly ARPU: $100.00</li>
    <li>Monthly Churn Rate: 2.5% (0.025)</li>
    <li>Average Customer Lifespan: 1 / 0.025 = 40 months</li>
    <li>Gross LTV: $100 / 0.025 = <strong>$4,000.00</strong></li>
</ul>

<h2>Why You Must Factor Gross Margin Into LTV</h2>

<p>Top-line revenue LTV can be deceptive. A software company with heavy server infrastructure or high customer success overhead cannot treat top-line revenue as spendable cash for customer acquisition.</p>

<p>Margin-adjusted Net LTV accounts for direct operational costs:</p>

<div style="background: var(--card-bg, #f8fafc); border: 1px solid var(--border, #e2e8f0); padding: 1.25rem; border-radius: 8px; font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.05rem; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  Net LTV = (ARPU x Gross Margin %) / Monthly Churn Rate
</div>

<p>If your SaaS gross margin is 80%, the net contribution per account is $4,000 x 0.80 = <strong>$3,200.00</strong>. This figure represents the absolute ceiling for customer acquisition spending.</p>

<h2>Evaluating the LTV to CAC Ratio</h2>

<p>To assess long-term viability, compare your Net LTV against your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):</p>

<ul>
    <li><strong>LTV:CAC Below 3:1:</strong> Unhealthy unit economics. Acquisition costs consume too much margin.</li>
    <li><strong>LTV:CAC At 3:1 to 5:1:</strong> Optimal benchmark for sustainable software growth.</li>
    <li><strong>LTV:CAC Above 5:1:</strong> Under-investing in growth channels. You can safely expand sales spending.</li>
</ul>
