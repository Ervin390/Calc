<h1>Gas Prices by State in 2026: Budgeting for Your Next Trip</h1>
<p style="color:#6b7280; font-size:0.9rem;">Published August 9, 2026 · 4 min read</p>

<p>Fuel prices in the United States vary dramatically depending on where you are filling up your tank. A driver in California frequently pays over $1.50 more per gallon than a driver in Mississippi for the exact same regular unleaded fuel. When planning a long-distance road trip, these state-by-state discrepancies can represent the difference between an affordable vacation and a major budget overrun.</p>

<p>Let's look at the current landscape of fuel prices across the country and check how to use this data to lower your travel expenses.</p>

<h2>Where Fuel Is Most Expensive</h2>

<p>The West Coast consistently holds the highest gas prices in the nation. California leads the pack with average regular unleaded hovering around $4.60 per gallon, driven by strict environmental formulations, high state fuel taxes, and refining constraints. Washington and Oregon are not far behind, with prices typically averaging between $4.10 and $4.30 per gallon.</p>

<p>Hawaii and Alaska also see high prices due to the logistics of transport. If you are road-tripping along the Pacific Coast, factor these higher rates into your planning calculations early.</p>

<h2>The Cheapest States to Fill Up</h2>

<p>The Gulf Coast and Southeast regions offer the most affordable fuel in the country. States like Mississippi, Texas, Louisiana, and Alabama benefit from proximity to major oil refineries and lower state fuel taxes. Gas in these states often costs between $2.90 and $3.15 per gallon.</p>

<p>If your route takes you through the South, planning your fuel stops to occur in these low-cost states rather than neighboring states can result in significant savings over a long trip.</p>

<h2>Tips for Gaming State Line Price Jumps</h2>

<p>The boundaries between states are often prime opportunities for savings. For example, crossing from California into Arizona or Nevada can immediately drop prices by up to $1.00 per gallon. Use navigation apps that show live station prices along your route to plan your stops before your tank is running on empty.</p>

<p>Filling up just before crossing into a high-cost state, or waiting to fill up until after you cross into a low-cost state, is one of the easiest ways to keep your travel costs low without changing your route.</p>
